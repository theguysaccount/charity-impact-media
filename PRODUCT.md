# PRODUCT

**User.** A US donor about to give, most acutely in the weeks around GivingTuesday (Dec 1) and year-end, who wants to know what a specific charity does with its money. Secondary: journalists and researchers who need sourced figures fast, and charity staff checking how their filing reads.

**Painful job.** "Before I give $100 to X, where does the money actually go, and who gets paid?" The answer is in X's Form 990, a 40–100-page tax return few donors can read. Ratings sites answer with a score or a paywall; the filing answers with lines and columns.

**Promise.** In five seconds: of every $100 X spent, how much went to programs, management and fundraising, from X's own filing, with the filing one click away. Then the rest: revenue sources, top pay by role, seven years of trends, governance answers, peers.

**Wedge.** The "where $100 goes" framing on every one of ~25,000 charity pages, built from public IRS data, refreshed as filings post, with no rating to argue with. Each page answers the queries donors type ("X CEO salary", "where does X money go", "is X a 501c3") and is citable by AI answer engines: plain factual sentences with a source link.

**Why now.** The IRS now publishes e-filed returns monthly and the GivingTuesday data lake serves them as individual files, so a machine can keep 25,000 breakdowns current for free. GivingTuesday 2026 is December 1.

**Activation metric.** A charity-page visitor who reaches the scorecard (`scorecard_seen`), counted once GoatCounter is enabled (OPERATIONS.md, key 3). Secondary: source-link clicks (`source_open`), shares, embeds, compares.

**First revenue event.** A sponsor or donation through the support link (key 2), once traffic exists. GitHub Pages rules out ads and paid features while hosted there.

**What v1 deliberately won't do.** Rate, grade or rank charities by merit; name individuals; consult for charities; cover small organizations (990-EZ/990-N) or private foundations; post on social media or contact anyone; judge program outcomes (the 990 doesn't report them).
